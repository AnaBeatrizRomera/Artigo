Opinion: Assistive Technology — Inclusion That Still Doesn't Reach Everyone

By Bia, Gabi e Mili | Published on May 2, 2025

Assistive technology aims to promote autonomy and inclusion for people with disabilities through resources such as screen readers, motorized wheelchairs, and communication software. However, it still doesn't reach everyone.

Economic barriers make many of these devices unaffordable for a large part of the population. In addition, geographical inequality means that remote areas have less access to equipment and technical support. Even when the technology is available, the lack of training and accessible information makes its full use difficult. Finally, prejudice and low political priority for accessibility further deepen this exclusion.

Therefore, although assistive technology has inclusive potential, it alone doesn't guarantee digital inclusion. Public policies, training, support, and efforts to reduce inequality are needed to ensure its benefits reach everyone.
